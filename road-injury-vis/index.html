The news counts deaths. Far more people end up in hospital.

Road safety news usually counts deaths, but far more people are admitted to hospital after road crashes than are killed in them.

This site explores who is hospitalised in Australia and how this changed from 2011 to 2021, with a focus on the most exposed road users: motorcyclists, pedal cyclists and pedestrians.

About the data

What the numbers count, where they come from, and the breaks to keep in mind.

  • Source

    Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE), Hospitalised Injury, September 2023 release, using hospital data from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW). Population for rates: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), estimated resident population at June each year.

  • What is counted

    Cases of hospital admission after a road crash, not counting cases that ended in death. A transfer to another hospital counts as one case, not two. The numbers are cases, not crashes or deaths.

  • Counting changes

    Hospitals changed how some admissions were counted around 2012 and 2017. These are marked on the trend chart.

  • Small numbers

    Some small counts are not published (n.p.) to protect privacy. These are shown as gaps, never as zero.

  • States

    Cases are counted by the state where the person lives. The states and territories add up to slightly less than the Australian total.
